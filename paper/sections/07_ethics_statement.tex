\section*{Ethical Statement}
\label{sec:ethics_statement}

This work investigates information diffusion, epistemic verification, and the tension between empirical truth and social deference in LLM agent societies. All simulations were conducted entirely \emph{in silico} within synthetic multi-agent environments; no human participants were involved, deceived, or subjected to misinformation. To proactively prevent dual-use risks and avoid producing actionable disinformation strategies, our experimental design purposefully evaluates benign, low-stakes cultural scenarios (fictitious community concert announcements featuring invented artists) rather than sensitive electoral, biomedical, or financial domains. As autonomous LLM agents are increasingly deployed in decentralized communication, digital governance, and collective consensus platforms, diagnosing vulnerabilities where agents sacrifice factual accuracy for interpersonal comfort or institutional deference is essential for engineering principled auditing tools, epistemic guardrails, and automated verification mechanisms.
