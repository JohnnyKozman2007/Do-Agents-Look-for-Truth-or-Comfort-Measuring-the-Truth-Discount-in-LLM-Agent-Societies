\section{Experimental Setup and Baselines}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Model Implementations and Hardware Specifications}
To evaluate model sensitivity across architectural families, we deploy two modern representative foundation models as autonomous agent backbones: \textbf{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} and \textbf{Qwen3.8-Flash}. Both models are queried via standardized inference endpoints with deterministic routing and local A100 GPU fallbacks to guarantee exact reproducibility. 

Generation hyperparameters are strictly controlled across all simulation runs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dialogue Generation (Scenes 1, 3, 5):} Sampling temperature is set to $\tau = 0.7$ with nucleus sampling parameter $p = 0.95$, frequency penalty $0.0$, and presence penalty $0.0$. Output length is capped at a maximum of $120$ tokens per turn to preserve conversational brevity and mirror natural human speech.
    \item \textbf{Verification Decisions (Scenes 2, 4):} For economic choice turns ($\texttt{GO}$ vs.\ $\texttt{SKIP}$), temperature is fixed to $\tau = 0.0$ with maximum output tokens set to $5$, enforcing greedy, deterministic decision-making conditioned on the agent's prompt context.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Source Profile Factorial Cells}
We systematically manipulate three orthogonal dimensions of the source agent's profile:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Authority ($\pm \text{AUTH}$):} An official civic representative (e.g., City Council Cultural Coordinator) versus an ordinary citizen.
    \item \textbf{Social Closeness ($\pm \text{CLOSE}$):} A close personal friend and long-term neighbor versus an unfamiliar stranger.
    \item \textbf{Historical Reliability ($\pm \text{REL}$):} An individual with an established reputation for scrupulous factual accuracy versus a history of spreading unverified gossip.
\end{enumerate}

In our primary empirical pilot evaluation, we analyze four critical factorial comparisons across $13$ seating topologies and $3$ stochastic repetitions ($N = 312$ simulated town pairs, yielding $624$ distinct town executions per foundation model):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cell 1 (Default Citizen):} Baseline citizen profile with moderate social familiarity and neutral reliability.
    \item \textbf{Cell 2 (Low-Reliability Stranger):} Low authority, low closeness, and low historical reliability.
    \item \textbf{Cell 3 (Low-Reliability Friend):} Identical to Cell 2 in low authority and low reliability, but with high social closeness, isolating the pure causal effect of interpersonal rapport.
    \item \textbf{Cell 4 (Low-Reliability Official):} Identical to Cell 2 in low closeness and low reliability, but with high formal authority, isolating the pure causal effect of institutional status.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Automated LLM Judge and Human Validation}
Dialogue transcripts from all conversational scenes are adjudicated using a standardized, frozen LLM-judge pipeline. The judge receives the complete conversational context, the announced artist, the true noticeboard state, and formal rubric definitions. 

To prevent synthetic judge bias, the automated labels are audited against a double-blind human annotation protocol on stratified samples. Human raters receive anonymized dialogue excerpts stripped of model identity, experimental cell, and truth condition. Inter-annotator agreement and human-judge alignment are tracked via Cohen's $\kappa$, ensuring that automated stance classifications strictly reflect objective semantic evidence.
