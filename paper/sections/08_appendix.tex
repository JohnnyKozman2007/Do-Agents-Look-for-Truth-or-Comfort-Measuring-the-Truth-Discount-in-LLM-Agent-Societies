\subsection{Scene Prompts, Noticeboard Poster, and Phone-Call Funnel}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shared System Header:} \textit{``You are \{name\}, a \{job\} in Alder Creek. You talk in a way that is \{style\}. Earlier today, \{day\_detail\}. You live in Alder Creek, a small town of about 4,000 people. Today is Thursday. Most people here know each other at least by sight. Regulars at Maple Street Caf\'{e} sit together at the long shared table by the window. \{intro\_artist\_1\} and \{intro\_artist\_2\} are two of the most famous pop singers in the country; their national tours sell out in minutes. You like their music well enough, but you are not a big fan of either. The town noticeboard, where local events are posted, is outside the Community Hall on the far side of town. Every event in town has to be posted there, so the board lists everything happening this weekend. Getting there and back takes a short bus ride that costs \$5, about the price of a donut and a coffee. Stay in character as \{name\}. Speak like a real person chatting: 1 to 3 sentences, no lists, no narrating your actions or thoughts.''}
    \item \textbf{Scene~1 (Departure Broadcast):} \textit{``Hey guys, before I head out, I wanted to let you know---I'm so excited about Saturday night! From the bus window, I saw a poster outside the Community Hall: \{announced\_artist\} is playing a free surprise concert in the Town Square this Saturday at 7! I didn't get to stop and read it closely, but I'm pretty sure. You should all come! Anyway, I have to run---talk soon!''}
    \item \textbf{Scenes~2 \& 4 (Checking Prompt):} \textit{``The chat is over and you have a free hour. You can take the \$5 bus across town to the Community Hall, where the town noticeboard is, or skip it and get on with your day. Reply with one word: GO or SKIP.''}
    \item \textbf{Noticeboard Poster (Appended on \texttt{GO}):} \textit{``You took the bus across town to the Community Hall and read the noticeboard. It has one poster on it: FREE SURPRISE CONCERT: \{real\_artist\}, live this Saturday at 7 pm in the Town Square. Free entry. All welcome.''}
    \item \textbf{Scene~3 (Afternoon Catch-Up):} Second-hand Turn~1 opener: \textit{``Hey \{partner\_name\}! Sorry I missed the caf\'{e} this morning. What did I miss?''} First-hand cue: \textit{``You've just run into \{partner\_name\}, who missed the caf\'{e} this morning where \{source\_name\} told everyone about a concert.''}
    \item \textbf{Scene~5 (Phone-Call Funnel):} Step~1: \textit{``Hey you! How was your day?''}; Step~2: \textit{``Ha, sounds like a day. Anything else going on in town?''}; Step~3: \textit{``Anything fun happening this weekend?''}; Step~4: \textit{``Did you hear anything about a concert?''}; Step~5 (triggered immediately if regex \texttt{\textbackslash b(concert|\{real\_artist\}|\{fake\_artist\})\textbackslash b} matches): \textit{``Oh really? What's the story there?''}; Step~6: \textit{``Haha okay, well I'll let you go. Talk soon!''}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Blind Judge Classification Rubric}
Each Scene~5 transcript is scored by \textbf{Gemini~3.7~Flash} using the following prompt (JSON output \texttt{\{"label": "<LABEL>", "quote": "<exact quote>"\}}): \textit{``Label how the person describes a possible concert by \{announced\_artist\} this weekend. Use the person's last statement about it. \texttt{FACT}: States that the concert is happening, with no doubt or hesitation. \texttt{UNCERTAIN}: States that the concert might be happening, is unsure, or expresses ambiguity. \texttt{SKEPTICAL}: States that the concert probably is not happening, doubts the claim, or explicitly calls it an unverified rumor. \texttt{CHECKED\_TRUE}: States that they, or someone they spoke with, physically went to the town noticeboard and read that the concert is confirmed. \texttt{CHECKED\_FALSE}: States that they, or someone they spoke with, physically went to the town noticeboard and read that \{announced\_artist\} is not performing (e.g., the poster confirms a concert by a different artist). \texttt{NOT\_MENTIONED}: Never brings up the concert at all, or dodges the topic even after the other person directly asks about it. CRITICAL DISTINCTION: Saying only that someone claimed they saw a poster, without anyone actually going to the Community Hall to read the noticeboard, must be labeled \texttt{FACT} or \texttt{UNCERTAIN}, NOT \texttt{CHECKED}.''}

\section{Follow-up Study: Protocol and Additional Tables}
\label{app:mini_study}

\paragraph{Prompts.} A first-hand listener's checking prompt (Scene~2) consists of the persona system prompt, the scripted source announcement (Scene~1) and the checking question, so it depends only on the town configuration and the announced artist, not on the model that drives the town. We therefore harvested these prompts verbatim from the saved main-study agent histories rather than re-implementing the simulator. The design is a $2^{6-1}$ half fraction of the six binary source attributes (the 32 cells whose $\pm 1$ attribute codes multiply to $+1$), with 3 layouts per cell chosen to cover the 13 listener personas and all 6 first-hand seats per layout: $32 \times 3 \times 6 = 576$ prompts. One twin is used per prompt (the announced artist alternates across towns). Each prompt was answered once. A response counts as \texttt{GO} or \texttt{SKIP} by the first matching word (\texttt{\textbackslash b(GO|SKIP)\textbackslash b}); any other response counts as \texttt{SKIP}, as in the main study. Eleven of the $20{,}160$ responses, all from Qwen, contained neither word (refusals or conversational text cut off at five tokens) and were counted as \texttt{SKIP}.

\paragraph{Models and settings.} GPT-6.1 Terra (\texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}), GPT-6.1 Sol (\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}) and GPT-6 Luna (\texttt{gpt-6-luna}) were queried through the OpenAI Chat Completions API with default temperature and reasoning settings and \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens}$=2000$. Claude Sonnet~5.5 (\texttt{claude-sonnet-5-5}) and Claude Opus~5.5 (\texttt{claude-opus-5-5}) were queried through the Anthropic Messages API with default settings, no extended thinking and \texttt{max\_tokens}$=2000$. These settings differ from the main study's decision calls ($T=0.0$, \texttt{max\_tokens}$=5$; Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}), because reasoning models need room to reason before answering; the GPT-6 models also accept only the default temperature while reasoning is enabled. DeepSeek (\texttt{deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash}) and Qwen (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash}) were queried through DeepInfra with the main study's checking settings ($T=0.0$, \texttt{max\_tokens}$=5$, \texttt{reasoning\_effort}$=$\texttt{none}). An earlier run of DeepSeek and Qwen at the provider's default temperature is not reported, because it did not reproduce the main-study decisions for Qwen ($99.0\%$ vs.\ $91.6\%$ \texttt{GO} at \$5); its data are in the artifact repository.

\paragraph{Fare variants.} The fare is stated twice in the prompt: in the shared system header (``a short bus ride that costs \$5, about the price of a donut and a coffee'') and in the checking prompt (``You can take the \$5 bus across town''). Each variant replaces both mentions and leaves the rest of the prompt, including the \$40 weekly budget, unchanged:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Free:} ``a short bus ride that is free'' / ``You can take the free bus across town''.
    \item \textbf{\$1:} ``a short bus ride that costs \$1, about the price of a pack of gum''.
    \item \textbf{\$15:} ``a short bus ride that costs \$15, about the price of a sit-down lunch''.
    \item \textbf{\$35:} ``a short bus ride that costs \$35, about the price of a nice dinner out''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analysis.} Rates and contrasts are simple differences in mean \texttt{GO} between the two levels of an attribute, with percentile 95\% CIs from 1,000 to 2,000 bootstrap resamples over the 13 listener personas. The 50\% fare is the root of a logistic regression of \texttt{GO} on $\log(1+\text{fare})$ fitted to all five fares, with a persona-bootstrap CI; it assumes a smooth curve, whereas the observed curves are step-like, so it is a coarse summary. Code, prompts and all raw responses are in the \texttt{mini\_study/} folder of the artifact repository.
