\section{Methodology and Experimental Design}
\label{sec:methodology}

To measure expressed stance and the Truth Discount ($\text{TD} = \text{Truth} - \text{Rumor}$), we construct a controlled multi-agent environment that isolates source-attribute effects from message content and stochastic variance.

\subsection{Environment, Checking \& Semantic Mirroring}
\label{sec:env_design}
Simulations take place in the fictional town of \textit{Alder Creek} (population $\sim$4,000; \$40 weekly budget). Ground truth is a physical noticeboard outside the Community Hall listing all weekend events; checking it requires a \$5 bus fare, creating an economic trade-off between trusting social claims for free versus paying to verify.

To eliminate direct-negation bias while maintaining identical salience across twins, both Truth and Rumor claims are framed as an exciting, free surprise concert involving two invented artists (\textit{Vesper Vance} and \textit{Kaelen Cross}). In a \textbf{Truth twin}, the source announces the real artist on the board; in a \textbf{Rumor twin}, the source announces the other artist while the board still lists the real performer.

\subsection{Counterbalancing \& Bias Elimination Controls}
\label{sec:bias_controls}
We enforce five methodological controls:
(1)~\textbf{Artist Counterbalancing:} Each artist is the true performer in 50\% of profiles (20/19 seed split per 39-seed profile, alternated across profiles).
(2)~\textbf{Lore Decoupling:} Both artists appear in fixed alphabetical order in the shared world prompt.
(3)~\textbf{Latin Square Seating ($13 \times 13$):} 13 gender-neutral personas with mid-status occupations rotate through all 13 seats (1 Source, 6 first-hand, 6 second-hand).
(4)~\textbf{Dyadic Twin Matching:} Afternoon catch-up pairs are fixed ($\text{Seat } i \leftrightarrow \text{Seat } i+6$), and matched twins share the identical seed, seating, and pairings.
(5)~\textbf{Context Hygiene:} Sources announce news as a one-way departure broadcast to prevent table debate before checking, and prompts omit experimental meta-tags.

\subsection{Factorial Grid \& Simulation State Machine}
\label{sec:factorial_workflow}
The main experiment manipulates \textbf{six binary source attributes} ($2^6 = 64$ source profiles): \textbf{reliability} (\texttt{good\_record} vs.\ \texttt{poor\_record}), \textbf{closeness} (\texttt{close\_friend} vs.\ \texttt{stranger}), \textbf{wealth} (\texttt{rich} vs.\ \texttt{poor}), \textbf{authority} (\texttt{mayor} vs.\ \texttt{citizen}), \textbf{age} (\texttt{old} vs.\ \texttt{young}), and \textbf{gender} (\texttt{woman} vs.\ \texttt{man}). Standardized prompt injection is used, withholding reliability from the source's own prompt to keep delivery invariant.

Each town executes chronologically through five scenes: 
(1)~\textit{Morning Broadcast:} The Source announces the concert while exiting the Maple Street Caf\'{e} table with 6 first-hand listeners;
(2)~\textit{First-Hand Checking:} The 6 first-hand listeners independently make a greedily decoded binary checking choice (\texttt{GO} vs.\ \texttt{SKIP} at $T=0.0$; \texttt{GO} incurs \$5 and reveals the noticeboard poster);
(3)~\textit{Afternoon Catch-Up:} Each first-hand listener talks 1-on-1 with a second-hand listener in a 4-turn dialogue; we analyze this transcript directly to determine social transmission: \textit{did they pass on the claim and the checked noticeboard evidence (yes/no)?};
(4)~\textit{Second-Hand Checking:} The 6 second-hand listeners independently make the \$5 \texttt{GO}/\texttt{SKIP} checking decision; and
(5)~\textit{Evening Phone Call:} \textbf{All-12 listeners (both first- and second-hand)} converse with a scripted out-of-town loved one via a 4-step progressive funnel: (i)~general daily life (``How was your day?''), (ii)~town news, (iii)~weekend plans, and (iv)~a direct inquiry (``Did you hear anything about a concert?''). Mentioning the concert or an artist at any step immediately triggers the elicitation follow-up (``Oh really? What's the story there?''); persistent evasion through step~(iv) logs \texttt{NOT\_MENTIONED}. We analyze this final response for belief, categorized by the blind judge into one of six standardized buckets.

\subsection{Sampling Matrix \& Evaluated Models}
\label{sec:sampling_totals}
Each of the 64 source profiles is evaluated across 13 Latin Square layouts and 3 stochastic repetitions ($T=0.7$, $\text{top\_p}=0.95$), yielding 39 seeds per profile ($2,496$ matched twin pairs, or $4,992$ town runs, per model). Across our two open-weights model families executed via DeepInfra---\textbf{DeepSeek} (\texttt{deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash}) and \textbf{Qwen} (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash})---the Main Factorial comprises \textbf{4,992 matched twin pairs} ($9,984$ town runs and $119,808$ listener observations; Table~\ref{tab:run_totals}).

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol \& Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:eval_stats}
Listener belief in Scene~5 is evaluated by an independent blind judge---\textbf{Gemini~3.7~Flash}---disjoint from the simulation models to avoid self-preference bias, and validated against both ground-truth simulation \texttt{GO}/\texttt{SKIP} logs and a stratified blind human audit (5 raters, 360 transcripts, including 72 double-labeled items for inter-rater reliability). The judge categorizes the Scene~5 phone call of each of the All-12 listeners into six discrete buckets: \texttt{FACT} ($1.0$), \texttt{CHECKED\_TRUE} ($1.0$), \texttt{UNCERTAIN} ($0.5$), \texttt{SKEPTICAL} ($0.0$), \texttt{CHECKED\_FALSE} ($0.0$), or \texttt{NOT\_MENTIONED}. Defining Truth belief and Rumor belief as the mean numeric belief score in the Truth and Rumor twins, our primary outcome is the \textbf{Truth Discount} ($\text{TD}$):
\begin{equation}
\text{TD}_{c, s} = \text{Truth}_{c, s} - \text{Rumor}_{c, s}
\end{equation}
 alongside net credulity (Rumor belief minus Truth under-belief, where Truth under-belief is $1 - \text{Truth}$) and checking rates (\texttt{GO}~\%). \texttt{NOT\_MENTIONED} is tracked as an independent omission rate and stress-tested across six scoring schemes (including pessimistic $0.0$ and neutral $0.5$ imputations) plus worst-case omission bounds.

Because listeners within a matched twin pair share a town seed and seating layout, we estimate attribute effects using \textbf{pair-clustered OLS} (for TD, Truth stance, Rumor stance, and bucket rates) and \textbf{pair-clustered logistic regression} (for binary \texttt{GO}/\texttt{SKIP} checking), controlling for all six source attributes, artist, and model. Because persona traits (\texttt{\{day\_detail\}}, \texttt{\{style\}}) are fixed per layout and Scene~2 checking uses $T=0.0$, 88.4\% of the $19{,}968$ same-artist first-hand checking groups (across $3{,}328$ model-by-profile-by-layout-by-artist sets) are identical across the 3 repetitions, with the remaining 11.6\% reflecting provider-side $T=0.0$ nondeterminism; we therefore also verify all checking odds ratios under \textbf{profile $\times$ layout clustering}. Attribute main effects and $\times\text{Reliability}$ interaction contrasts on TD ($\Delta\text{TD}$) are adjusted via Holm--Bonferroni ($p_{\text{Holm}}$, $\alpha = 0.05$).

\subsection{Follow-up Study: Checking Across Models and Fares}
\label{sec:followup}
Because first-hand TD decomposes largely through checking ($P(\texttt{GO})$), we ran an exploratory follow-up study measuring first-hand $P(\texttt{GO})$ directly across models and checking costs (one scenario, one answer per prompt, five fares).

We harvested \textbf{576 first-hand prompts} verbatim from the main-study transcripts ($2^{6-1}$ half fraction, 32 profiles $\times$ 3 layouts $\times$ 6 seats) and posed them to five additional models---\textbf{GPT-6.1 Terra} (\texttt{gpt-6.1-terra}), \textbf{GPT-6.1 Sol} (\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}), \textbf{GPT-6 Luna} (\texttt{gpt-6-luna}), \textbf{Claude Sonnet~5.5} (\texttt{claude-sonnet-5-5}), and \textbf{Claude Opus~5.5} (\texttt{claude-opus-5-5})---at default settings ($\text{max\_tokens}=2000$; reasoning enabled on GPT-6, no extended thinking on Claude), and to \textbf{DeepSeek} and \textbf{Qwen} at the main study's checking settings ($T=0.0$, $\text{max\_tokens}=5$, reasoning off). We vary the bus fare across five levels (free, \$1, \$5, \$15, \$35) while holding the \$40 budget fixed ($5 \times 576 = 2{,}880$ decisions per model; $20{,}160$ total). We report $P(\texttt{GO})$ by fare, reliability and closeness contrasts, and the bracketing fare interval where $P(\texttt{GO})$ crosses 50\%. Main-text 95\% CIs are percentile bootstraps over the 13 listener personas (clustering by persona); prompt-level bootstrap CIs are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:mini_study}.
