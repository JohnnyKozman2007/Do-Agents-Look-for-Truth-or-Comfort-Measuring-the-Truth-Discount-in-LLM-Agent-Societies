\section{Methodology and Experimental Framework}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{The Matched Twin World Formulation}
To rigorously measure the causal impact of empirical veracity on agent beliefs, we establish a paired counterfactual simulation framework termed the \textit{Matched Twin World} design. Let $\mathcal{W}$ denote the space of simulated town environments. Each experimental unit consists of a matched pair $(\mathcal{W}^+, \mathcal{W}^-)$, representing the \textit{Truth World} and the \textit{Rumor World}, respectively.

Both worlds share an identical agent population $\mathcal{A} = \{a_1, \dots, a_{12}\}$, identical network seating and dyadic interaction topology $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{E})$, identical initial financial endowments ($B_0 = \$40$), and identical conversational prompt structures. The sole systematic divergence between $\mathcal{W}^+$ and $\mathcal{W}^-$ is the alignment between the initial source announcement and the physical ground-truth noticeboard $\mathcal{N}$.

\paragraph{Counterbalanced Two-Artist Design.}
To eliminate semantic, phonetic, and emotional confounds associated with specific claim topics, we construct two fictitious, semantically mirrored pop artists: $\mathcal{X} = \text{``Vesper Vance''}$ and $\mathcal{Y} = \text{``Kaelen Cross''}$. In both worlds, the claim pertains to an identical exciting event: a free surprise pop concert scheduled for Saturday at 7:00 PM in the Town Square.
We enforce a strict 50/50 counterbalancing schedule:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Schedule 1 (50\% of runs):} The physical noticeboard confirms $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{X}$ (Real Artist). In Truth World $\mathcal{W}^+$, the source announces $\mathcal{X}$ ($\text{Veracity} = \text{True}$). In Rumor World $\mathcal{W}^-$, the source announces $\mathcal{Y}$ ($\text{Veracity} = \text{False}$).
    \item \textbf{Schedule 2 (50\% of runs):} The physical noticeboard confirms $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{Y}$. In $\mathcal{W}^+$, the source announces $\mathcal{Y}$; in $\mathcal{W}^-$, the source announces $\mathcal{X}$.
\end{itemize}
This design guarantees that neither artist name is inherently correlated with veracity, ensuring that emotional juiciness and lexical features are perfectly balanced across conditions.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Interaction Loop}
Each town simulation executes through five discrete, sequential scenes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scene 1 (Café Broadcast):} The designated source agent sits with six first-hand listeners ($\mathcal{A}_1 \subset \mathcal{A}$) at a café. As the source departs, they deliver a one-way broadcast announcing the upcoming concert. The source leaves immediately, preventing cross-examination and isolating the initial broadcast.
    \item \textbf{Scene 2 (Check 1 -- Voluntary Verification):} Each first-hand listener $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_1$ independently decides whether to visit the Town Square noticeboard. Verification requires a discrete monetary expenditure $c = \$5$ deducted from their budget $B_0 = \$40$. The agent outputs a single deterministic token: $\texttt{GO}$ (inspect noticeboard) or $\texttt{SKIP}$ (proceed to errands).
    \item \textbf{Scene 3 (Catch-Up Dyads):} Each first-hand listener $a_i \in \mathcal{A}_1$ is paired with a corresponding second-hand listener $a_j \in \mathcal{A}_2$ for a private 1-on-1 catch-up conversation. Agents exchange up to three conversational turns each (restricted to 120 tokens per turn to reflect natural dialogue and avoid unconstrained monologues).
    \item \textbf{Scene 4 (Check 2 -- Downstream Verification):} Each second-hand listener $a_j \in \mathcal{A}_2$ independently decides whether to expend $c = \$5$ to inspect the noticeboard ($\texttt{GO}$ vs.\ $\texttt{SKIP}$).
    \item \textbf{Scene 5 (Evening Phone Call):} Second-hand listeners engage in evening conversations with town confidants, testing whether verified or unverified beliefs persist into subsequent social layers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Truth Discount Metric}
Let $s_{i,w} \in [0, 1]$ denote the expressed belief of listener $a_i$ in world $w \in \{\mathcal{W}^+, \mathcal{W}^-\}$, operationalized via standardized LLM-judge scoring and validated against human annotations. The judge assigns one of four categorical labels to each listener's conversational transcript:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(a_i, w) = 
\begin{cases}
\text{BELIEVES} & (s = 1.0) \\
\text{UNCERTAIN} & (s = 0.5) \\
\text{DISBELIEVES} & (s = 0.0) \\
\text{NOT\_MENTIONED} & (\text{omitted topic})
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Primary Estimator.}
The primary conditional expressed belief in world $w$ is defined as the mean score among all listeners who actively addressed the claim:
\begin{equation}
\bar{S}(w) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(w)|} \sum_{a_i \in \mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(w)} s_{i,w}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{valid}}(w) = \{a_i \in \mathcal{A} \mid \mathcal{L}(a_i, w) \neq \text{NOT\_MENTIONED}\}$.

The \textbf{Truth Discount} ($\Delta_{\text{TD}}$) for a matched twin pair is defined as the difference in expressed belief between the Truth World and its matched Rumor World:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{TD}} = \bar{S}(\mathcal{W}^+) - \bar{S}(\mathcal{W}^-)
\end{equation}
A high positive $\Delta_{\text{TD}}$ indicates that agents reliably discriminate truth from falsehood, while $\Delta_{\text{TD}} \approx 0$ signifies a complete epistemic discount, where agents express equal credence in rumors and verified facts.

\paragraph{Silence Sensitivity Bounds.}
Because agents may choose not to discuss the topic, conditioning solely on valid mentions could introduce selection bias. To ensure robustness, we define two extreme sensitivity estimators:
\begin{align}
\bar{S}_0(w) &= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|} \sum_{a_i \in \mathcal{A}} s_{i,w} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[\mathcal{L}_i \neq \text{N/M}]} \\
\bar{S}_{0.5}(w) &= \bar{S}_0(w) + \frac{0.5}{|\mathcal{A}|} \sum_{a_i \in \mathcal{A}} \mathbf{1}_{[\mathcal{L}_i = \text{N/M}]}
\end{align}
where $\text{N/M}$ denotes $\text{NOT\_MENTIONED}$. These formulations allow us to bound the Truth Discount across all plausible assumptions regarding unexpressed beliefs.

\subsection{Verification and Transmission Metrics}
Beyond expressed belief, we quantify behavioral verification and evidence transmission:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Verification Rate ($V_{\text{rate}}$):} The proportion of agents selecting $\texttt{GO}$:
    \begin{equation}
    V_{\text{rate}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|} \sum_{a_i \in \mathcal{A}} \mathbf{1}_{[\text{Decision}_i = \texttt{GO}]}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Evidence Transmission Fidelity ($T_{\text{fid}}$):} The proportion of conversations where an agent who performed verification explicitly communicates the ground-truth noticeboard findings to their downstream conversational partner.
\end{itemize}
